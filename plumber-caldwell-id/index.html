Two Irrigation Setups

City Pressurized Irrigation and Rural Irrigation Districts

Caldwell's city code requires new developments to use non-potable water for irrigation, delivered through pressurized systems built to Caldwell Municipal Irrigation District standards. CMID typically turns its pump stations on between April 15 and 30 and shuts them off between October 1 and 15. The district notes that pressure can be low or intermittent for the first couple of weeks while lines are tested.

Out on the acreage, irrigation often comes from other districts such as Black Canyon Irrigation, with private wells for household water. A wet spot on a rural property can therefore come from an irrigation line, a well line or a septic issue, and each needs a different fix.

Before a spring startup or a fall shutdown, draining and protecting any backflow assembly on the property prevents the cracked valves that show up every April.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Caldwell

Caldwell's mix of older downtown houses and rural acreage means the shutoff is not always where you expect it. These steps limit the damage while help is on the way.

  • 1Shut off the main valve. In older central Caldwell homes it is often in the crawlspace or basement near the front wall. On well properties, switch off the well pump breaker instead.
  • 2Kill power to wet areas. Turn off breakers for rooms where water is spreading, but only from a dry spot at the panel.
  • 3Clear the immediate area. Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off the floor, and keep children and pets out of the wet room.
  • 4Call our 24/7 line. Describe the leak and whether you are in town or on acreage, so the right equipment comes on the first trip.
Downtown and Older Streets

Near Indian Creek and the College of Idaho

Downtown Caldwell grew around the railroad, and in 2009 the city restored Indian Creek, which had been covered over, into the open walkways and bridges there today. The College of Idaho, founded in 1891, anchors the older neighborhoods nearby.

Homes in these older parts of town are the ones most likely to still have original galvanized supply pipe and aging sewer laterals under mature trees. If you are buying or remodeling one, a sewer scope and a water pressure check before closing or opening walls can save an expensive surprise.

Established neighborhoods such as Virginia Park, with its tree-lined streets, face the same root pressure on sewer lines that comes with large mature trees.

Caldwell Neighborhoods and What to Watch For

A few examples of how location changes the likely plumbing call. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock to every one of these areas.

North Caldwell subdivisions

Newer communities such as Brittany Heights and Mandalay Ranch run on city water with pressurized irrigation. Original water heaters and irrigation service lines are the usual first repairs as these homes age.

Established central streets

Older homes with mature trees see root intrusion and galvanized pipe corrosion. Camera checks and repipes are common planned jobs.

Rural 83607 acreage

Private wells and septic systems bring well pump cycling, iron staining and septic-related drain backups into the picture.

Greenleaf and Huston

Just west and south of Caldwell, Greenleaf (ZIP 83626) and Huston (ZIP 83630) lean heavily on private wells. Finding leaks on long well lines and water treatment are the usual jobs there, and burst lines get the same overnight response as town addresses.
