Old Nampa Neighborhood Historic District

Plumber in Old Nampa, Nampa, ID

The Old Nampa Neighborhood Historic District sits a few blocks south of downtown, roughly between 4th and 11th Avenues South and 4th and 9th Streets South. The City of Nampa calls it the city's largest historic district, with about 243 homes.

Most of those homes went up between 1920 and 1940, and many still have original built-ins and fixtures. Plumbing work here goes best when it fixes the problem without tearing out what makes the house worth keeping.

Where It Is

Old Nampa on the Map

The National Register listing describes the district as roughly bounded by 4th Avenue South, 4th Street South, 11th Avenue South and 9th Street South. A local news report on the city's preservation work put it at about 18 blocks, a short walk from downtown Nampa.

The district was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on March 21, 2007. Exact edges matter less for plumbing than the age of the house in front of you, so treat the boundary streets as a guide rather than a hard line.

The Houses

Bungalows, Kit Homes and Original Fixtures

The City of Nampa describes most homes here as craftsman bungalows built from plan books and kit catalogs, with some Queen Anne, Dutch Colonial Revival and Prairie School houses mixed in. It also notes that many homes keep original windows, built-ins and fixtures.

A local report on the district found that the earliest homes, from around 1910, line 11th Avenue South between 5th and 9th Streets South, and that only 13 new houses were built in the district over the past 60 years. That makes it one of the most intact older neighborhoods in Nampa.

None of that says what pipe is inside a particular house. Many homes this old have had their plumbing updated at least once, so the only reliable way to know is to look at the pipe where it is exposed, at the water heater, under sinks and in the crawlspace.

Before Any Remodel

Planning Plumbing Work in a Historic Old Nampa House

The district is listed on the National Register, and the City of Nampa has been working toward local historic designation and design guidelines. Plumbing is mostly hidden, but these steps keep a project on track.

  • 1Find the main shutoff first. Older homes sometimes have the valve in the crawlspace or near the front foundation. Know where it is before anything is opened.
  • 2Scope the sewer lateral. A camera look before new bathrooms or a kitchen goes in shows whether the line to the street can take the added fixtures.
  • 3Ask about fixture-friendly routes. New supply lines can often run through the crawlspace and closets, which limits how much plaster and trim has to come off.
  • 4Check with the city on exterior work. If a project touches the outside of the house, ask the City of Nampa's historic preservation staff whether any guidelines apply.
Cost and Care

What Shapes the Cost of Plumbing Work in an Older House

In a house of this age, cost depends mostly on access and parts. Pipe that runs through an open crawlspace is quicker to reach than pipe behind plaster walls, and a vintage fixture may need parts that take time to find. Ask for the price before any work starts, and ask whether a repair or a replacement makes more sense for that fixture.

Avoid pouring caustic drain cleaners into older drains. They are hazardous to handle, can damage some pipe and rarely clear a blockage deep in the line. A cable or a camera look is the safer first step.
