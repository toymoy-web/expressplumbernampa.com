Water Damage Restoration in Nampa, ID

Water damage restoration takes a home from wet and damaged back to normal: removing the water, drying the structure, replacing what cannot be saved and checking that no hidden moisture is left to grow mold. For homes in Nampa, Idaho, the line at (833) 521-1033 connects you with water damage restoration help 24/7.

Restoration is a different job from fixing the pipe. A plumber stops the leak. Restoration deals with what the water soaked on its way out: drywall, insulation, flooring, cabinets and belongings.

What to Do in the First Hour After Water Damage

  • 1Stop the source. Close the main water shutoff. If the cause is a burst line, our plumbing team can fix the broken pipe on a separate line.
  • 2Stay safe around electricity. Do not walk through standing water near outlets or appliances. Shut off power to affected areas only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot.
  • 3Photograph before moving anything. Document the source, water levels and damaged items for your insurer.
  • 4Move what you can to a dry area. Lift furniture onto blocks or foil, and move rugs, electronics and papers.
  • 5Do not use a household vacuum on water. It is an electrical hazard and will not remove enough water to matter.
Water Types

Clean, Gray and Black Water Are Handled Differently

The IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration sorts water by contamination level, because it changes what can be dried and what must be removed.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means
Category 1, clean waterBurst supply line, water heater leakMany materials can be dried in place if acted on quickly
Category 2, gray waterWasher overflow, dishwasher, toilet overflow without wasteMore materials removed, cleaning required
Category 3, black waterSewer backup, flooding from outsidePorous materials removed, sanitizing required

Clean water does not stay clean. After a day or two in carpet and drywall, it can become gray water, which is one more reason speed matters.

The Process

From Soaked to Restored

Restoration starts with water mitigation: extracting standing water, removing what cannot be saved and setting up drying equipment. That stage stops the damage from spreading.

Drying is then monitored with moisture meters until materials reach normal levels. Drying by the calendar instead of by measurement is how hidden moisture gets sealed behind new drywall.

Once dry, the restoration phase replaces drywall, insulation, baseboards, flooring and cabinetry as needed. Where mold has started, the IICRC S520 standard guides remediation before rebuilding.

1. MitigateExtract and stabilize
2. DryMonitor with meters
3. RemoveUnsalvageable materials
4. RestoreRebuild and finish
Nampa Homes

Where Water Damage Usually Comes From Here

The City of Nampa rates floods from outside as rare, so most water damage here starts inside the house. Winter burst pipes in crawlspaces and exterior walls lead the list, followed by failed water heaters, washer and toilet supply lines, and sewer backups.

Crawlspace homes, common across the Treasure Valley, add a specific risk: a leak under the house can soak insulation and raise humidity for days before anyone notices. If the source was a plumbing failure, fixing it properly matters as much as drying, and our leak-locating crew can confirm nothing else is leaking before walls are closed.

We serve homes throughout Nampa and nearby towns, including Kuna.

Insurance and Cost

What Drives the Cost of Restoration

Area affected, water category and how long the water sat drive most of the cost. Quick response keeps more materials salvageable.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden water damage, such as a burst pipe, and exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside. Sewer backup coverage usually needs a separate endorsement. Keep photos and invoices, and call your insurer early.
