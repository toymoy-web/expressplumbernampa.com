Tankless Descaling, Repair and Installation

Tankless Water Heater Service in Nampa, ID

Tankless water heater service in Nampa, Idaho mostly comes down to two things: keeping mineral scale out of the heat exchanger, and fixing the sensors, igniters and flow parts that stop a unit from firing. We handle both, along with new tankless installs and conversions from a tank.

A tankless heater heats water only as it flows through, so it never runs out the way a 50-gallon tank does. That same design makes it sensitive to scale, gas supply and venting in ways a tank is not, which is why annual service matters more here than on a conventional heater.

Sound Familiar?

Error Codes, Cold Bursts and Other Signs Your Tankless Needs Service

Most people do not search for descaling. They notice a symptom first. The display flashes an error code. Hot water turns lukewarm when a second shower starts. The unit fires, then cuts out. Or it has simply been several years since anyone opened the service valves.

Each of those has a short list of causes. Scale on the heat exchanger triggers overheat and flow errors. A dirty inlet screen restricts flow until the unit will not ignite on low-flow fixtures. A failing flame rod or igniter gives the fire-then-shut-off pattern. Undersized gas supply shows up as lost heat when demand climbs.

Some companies will only offer to replace a brand they do not usually service. We diagnose first. Replacement is only on the table when the heat exchanger itself has failed.

Descaling

How a Tankless Flush Works

A proper install includes isolation valves, sometimes called service valves, on the hot and cold connections. We close them, connect a small pump and hoses, and circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set period before flushing it clean and cleaning the inlet screen.

If your unit was installed without isolation valves, we add them during the first service visit. It is a one-time fix that makes every future flush faster and cheaper.

Nampa's city wells range from soft to about 7 grains per gallon, and homes at the harder end benefit most from a yearly flush. If scale keeps returning quickly, pairing the tankless unit with water softening protects the heat exchanger.

Sizing

Why a Tankless Unit Feels Weaker in Winter

Tankless heaters are sized by flow rate at a given temperature rise. Groundwater arrives colder in winter, so the unit has to raise the temperature further, and the flow it can deliver at full heat drops.

Converting from a Tank

What a Tank-to-Tankless Switch Involves

A tankless unit is not a drop-in replacement for a tank. These are the items that decide whether a conversion is simple or involved.

  • 1Gas line size. Tankless gas units draw much more gas at full fire than a tank, so the supply line often needs upsizing.
  • 2Venting. Condensing units vent through PVC and need a condensate drain, with a neutralizer where required.
  • 3Electrical. Gas tankless units still need a standard outlet for controls and freeze protection.
  • 4Mounting location. Wall space near the old tank location keeps piping changes small.
  • 5Rebate eligibility. Intermountain Gas has offered rebates on tankless units rated 0.91 UEF or higher. Check current terms before choosing a model.

Do It Yourself?

What Owners Can Safely Do Between Service Visits

Owners can clean the cold water inlet screen, check that the exterior vent termination is clear of snow or debris, and clean faucet aerators that have scaled up. Those three steps fix a surprising share of low-flow and ignition complaints.

Descaling with a pump kit is possible for handy owners if the unit has service valves, but gas, venting and internal electrical parts are jobs for a qualified technician.

If scale returns quickly after each flush, softening the water supply protects the heat exchanger far better than more frequent descaling.
