Whole-House and Drinking Water Filtration

Water Filtration Installation in Nampa, ID

Water filtration installation in Nampa, Idaho starts with a simple question: what do you want to remove, and from which taps? A whole-house filter treats every fixture for taste, odor and sediment. An under-sink reverse osmosis system treats drinking and cooking water to a much finer level. Many homes use one of each.

We test before recommending anything, install systems certified to the relevant NSF/ANSI standards, and explain the maintenance each one needs. We do not make health claims. Questions about health effects belong with your doctor or the health district.

Sound Familiar?

Chlorine Taste, Cloudy Water or Questions About What Is in the Tap

People call about filtration for different reasons. The water tastes of chlorine. Ice cubes come out cloudy. A new baby has parents reading the city's water report closely. Or a rural well has never been tested at all.

Nampa's drinking water comes from city wells drawing on the Snake River Plain aquifer. The city's annual reports state that the water meets EPA and state standards, and they also note that it contains low levels of naturally occurring arsenic. The same reports advise that nitrate above 10 parts per million is a risk for infants under six months, and that lead, if present, typically comes from service lines and home plumbing rather than the source water.

What that means in practice: read your latest city report, decide what you want to reduce, then choose a filter certified to reduce that specific thing. Filters are not interchangeable.

Private Wells

Well Water Is a Different Starting Point

The city's water report covers only the city system. Homes on private wells, which are common on acreage outside Nampa city limits, are responsible for testing their own water, and results can differ a lot from city water.

Wells in farm country can carry nitrate, iron, hardness well above city levels, and sometimes bacteria. A lab test tells you what you are dealing with, and treatment is chosen from the results. Rural properties south of town, including around rural Melba, are where we see the widest range of well water.

A well test before installing any system avoids paying for treatment you do not need, or missing one you do.

City water: read the annual water quality report.
Private well: owner tests, results vary widely.
Treatment chosen from test results, not guesses.
Maintenance

What to Do to Keep a Filter Working

  • 1Change cartridges on schedule. A spent carbon filter stops doing its job and can shed what it collected. Put the dates on a calendar.
  • 2Replace RO membranes as rated. Most reverse osmosis systems need a membrane change every few years and pre-filters more often.
  • 3Protect RO membranes from hard water. On harder Nampa wells, softening ahead of an RO unit extends membrane life.
  • 4Sanitize after long absences. A system that sat unused for weeks should be flushed before drinking from it.
  • 5Keep the bypass reachable. A whole-house filter should have a bypass valve so water keeps flowing during service.
