Repiping

Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Nampa, ID

Repiping replaces the water supply lines inside a house, usually because the old pipe has corroded, narrowed or started leaking in more than one place. In Nampa, Idaho, most whole-home repipes we quote are older houses near downtown still running original galvanized steel, plus a smaller number of homes where an earlier plastic pipe has become unreliable.

The goal is simple: one planned project instead of a string of emergency patches. We map the existing lines, give you a written scope, pull the permit and pressure-test the new system before any wall is closed.

Is It Time?

Rusty Water, Weak Showers and Repeat Leaks: Signs the Pipe Itself Is Failing

Nobody searches for a repipe first. They notice brown water after a vacation, a shower that barely runs when the washer fills, or a third pinhole leak in two years. Those are the symptoms of pipe that is corroding from the inside.

Galvanized steel is the usual cause in older Nampa homes. Its zinc coating wears away over decades, the steel underneath rusts, and the rust builds up inside the pipe until the opening is a fraction of its original size. Pressure drops first at the fixtures farthest from the meter, and leaks appear at threaded joints where the wall is thinnest.

A single leak in otherwise sound pipe is a repair. Low pressure throughout the house plus discolored water plus more than one leak is a pattern, and patching one spot on a failing system usually moves the next leak a few feet down the line.

Materials

PEX or Copper for a Nampa Repipe

Both materials are approved under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho's plumbing code is based on. The better choice depends on your house, not on a sales pitch.

FactorPEXCopper
Installed costLowerHigher
Wall openings neededFewer, because it bends around framingMore, because runs need fittings at turns
Freeze toleranceBetter, it can expand slightly before splittingSplits more readily when water freezes inside
Hard water scaleResists buildup on the pipe wallCan pit in some water chemistry over time
Exposure to sunlightMust be protected from UVNot affected

For most Nampa repipes, PEX wins on cost, fewer drywall cuts and freeze tolerance in crawlspaces. Copper still makes sense where lines are exposed or where an owner simply prefers it.

What It Involves

How a Repipe Actually Gets Done

Most single-family repipes take a few working days. The crew runs new lines through attics, crawlspaces and walls, connects each fixture, then ties the new system in at the main and the water heater. Water is usually off only during the working hours of the tie-in days.

Wall openings are kept small and grouped where possible. Patching drywall is often handled as a separate step, and we will tell you up front which parts of the finish work are in our scope.

A repipe is also the right moment to fix what the old system got wrong: a missing thermal expansion tank, a failing pressure-reducing valve or a main shutoff that no longer closes. If the pipe from the street is also galvanized, that is a separate job covered on our page about the buried main line, because the service line runs outside the house.

When a Repipe Is Not the Answer

Not every house with a leak needs new pipe. If the leak is at a single fitting on otherwise healthy copper or PEX, or if pressure is low because of a failing pressure-reducing valve, a targeted repair is the honest recommendation.

When the cause is unclear, start with leak detection and a pressure check. That costs far less than a repipe and tells you whether the pipe is actually the problem.

Getting Ready

What to Do Before Your Repipe Starts

  • 1Clear access points. Move furniture and stored items away from walls near bathrooms, the kitchen and laundry, and clear the path to the crawlspace or attic hatch.
  • 2Protect valuables. Take down framed pictures and fragile items on walls that share plumbing, since drywall gets cut and patched.
  • 3Plan around water-off hours. Fill a few jugs for drinking and cooking on tie-in days, and plan laundry and showers for evenings.
  • 4Secure pets. Crews move in and out through doors and access hatches all day.
  • 5Confirm finish work. Agree in writing on who patches and paints drywall before the job starts, so there are no surprises.
Pricing

What Sets the Cost of Repiping a House

Repipe cost follows the size of the house, the number of fixtures and how easy the walls and ceilings are to reach.

Homeowner insurance typically does not pay for replacing old pipe, though it may cover sudden water damage from a leak. Some owners schedule a repipe right after a claim to avoid a second one.

Signs Checklist

Signs It Is Time to Stop Repairing and Repipe

Several of these together usually tip the decision: more than one leak in the past two years, brown water after the water has sat, low pressure at every fixture rather than one, visible corrosion on exposed pipe, and a house still running galvanized or polybutylene supply lines.

If only one of these applies, targeted repairs and a pressure check may be enough. If three or more apply, a repipe quote is worth getting before the next leak. Having someone find the current leak first keeps the emergency repair small while you decide.
