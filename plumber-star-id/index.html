Private Wells

Nitrate and the Northeast Star Priority Area

The Idaho Department of Environmental Quality has designated a Northeast Star Nitrate Priority Area and in 2025 invited private well owners there to take part in a sampling event. For anyone on a private well near Star, that is a clear signal to test.

City water systems are tested by the provider. Private wells are the owner's responsibility, and a lab test is the only way to know what is in the water. If nitrate shows up, reverse osmosis systems certified for nitrate reduction are the common point-of-use choice. Our drinking water treatment page covers the options, and we do not make health claims about results.

For households with infants, the nitrate question matters most. Ask your health care provider about infant water if your test shows high nitrate.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Star

Star's young houses usually put the main shutoff in the garage beside the water heater or a tankless unit on the wall. A few quick steps keep a leak from reaching new flooring.

  • 1Shut off the main valve. Find it in the garage or utility room and close it fully. For a leaking tankless unit, also close its service valves.
  • 2Kill power to wet areas. Switch off breakers for flooded rooms from a dry spot, and unplug a tankless unit only if its outlet is dry.
  • 3Clear the immediate area. Move rugs, electronics and furniture off wet floors, since new laminate and carpet soak up water quickly.
  • 4Call our 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher which county side you are on and whether the home is on city water or a private well.

New Owners

What to Do in Your First Year in a New Star Home

A few minutes of setup now makes any future plumbing emergency shorter and cheaper.

  • 1Find and label the main shutoff. In many newer homes it is in the garage or near the water heater. Make sure everyone in the house knows where it is and that it turns.
  • 2Locate the sewer cleanout. Usually a capped pipe near the foundation outside, required to be accessible under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Keep it clear of landscaping so it can be reached in a backup.
  • 3Identify your irrigation valve. Know which valve is yours and which belongs to the irrigation system operator, and never operate theirs.
  • 4Register the water heater. Record the model, serial number and install date. Manufacturer warranties often depend on it.
  • 5Test a private well. If you are on a well, test before your first summer, and especially if you are near the Northeast Star Nitrate Priority Area.
