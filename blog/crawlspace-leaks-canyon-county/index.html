Crawlspace Leaks in Canyon County Homes: Signs to Watch and What to Check

Written by the Nampa Express Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026

A lot of Canyon County homes sit over crawlspaces rather than basements or slabs. That puts supply lines, drains and often the main shutoff in a space most owners rarely enter. A leak there can run for weeks before anyone sees water.

This guide covers the signs that something is leaking under the house, how to check without getting hurt, and what the common causes are.

Crawlspaces also make leaks easy to ignore. Most owners enter theirs rarely, maybe once when buying the house and again when something goes wrong. A few minutes with a flashlight twice a year, at the access hatch if nowhere else, catches most slow leaks while they are still cheap to fix and before insulation and subfloor have soaked up weeks of water.

Safe Checking

What to Do Before Going Into the Crawlspace

Crawlspaces can be tight, dark and occasionally hazardous. A few precautions matter.

  • 1Turn off the water if you see active leaking from the hatch. Use the main shutoff, which in many crawlspace homes is near the access point.
  • 2Bring a good light and wear coveralls, gloves and a mask. Old insulation and dust are unpleasant, and damp spaces can hold mold.
  • 3Watch for electrical hazards. Do not touch wiring or junction boxes if there is standing water.
  • 4Look, do not disturb. Note wet insulation, puddles, dripping pipes and wet soil, and photograph them.
  • 5Get out if anything seems unsafe. Sewage, heavy mold or deep water are jobs for professionals.
Causes

What Usually Leaks Under the House

Supply lines leak at fittings, especially where pipes were stressed by freezing in a past winter. Drain lines leak at old joints or where a trap or cleanout has worked loose. Water heater relief valve lines routed under the floor can drip without anyone noticing.

Freezing is the most common trigger. Crawlspace vents let winter air across pipes, and a small split from January can drip quietly for months. Insulating crawlspace lines is the most effective prevention, and fixing lines that froze covers the winter side.

In spring, irrigation water outside can also raise moisture near the foundation, which is why confirming whether water is coming from plumbing or from outside matters.

Next Steps

Fixing It and Drying It Out

The first job is finding the exact source. A crawlspace leak can travel along a pipe or joist before dripping, so the wet spot is not always under the leak. Locating the leak professionally isolates the supply side with the meter and pressure tests before anything is cut.

Once repaired, a wet crawlspace needs to dry. Soaked insulation usually has to come out, and standing water needs removal. For significant water, a restoration crew handles extraction and drying, which is a separate service from the plumbing repair.

Many newer homes in towns like Middleton are built over crawl spaces too, so a yearly look under the house is worthwhile at any age.

Routine Check

A Twice-a-Year Crawlspace Walk-Through

In fall, check that pipe insulation is intact, especially near vents and the access hatch, and look for any drips before winter. In spring, look again for anything that split or loosened during cold weather, and check that the soil is dry.

Take a few photos each time. Comparing them over the years makes slow changes such as sagging insulation, new stains or rising moisture much easier to spot.

If your home has a vapor barrier on the soil, check that it is intact and not holding puddles on top, which usually means water is coming from above.

Leak or Drainage

Plumbing Leak or Outside Water: How to Tell

Water under a house comes from plumbing or from outside. The fix is very different, so it is worth sorting out first.

CluePoints to plumbingPoints to outside water
Water meter moves with all fixtures offYesNo
Wet area appears after rain or irrigationNoYes
Water is warm or steaming in winterYes, a hot water lineNo
Wetness concentrated under a bathroom or kitchenLikelyLess likely
Wetness along one foundation wallPossibleLikely, check gutters and grading
Sewer smellYes, a drain lineNo

If outside water is the cause, gutters, downspouts, yard grading and irrigation spray against the foundation are the usual culprits, and a plumber may not be the right first call. If the clues point to plumbing, pinpointing the leak before anything is opened keeps the repair small.
