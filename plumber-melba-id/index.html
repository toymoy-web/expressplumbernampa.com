Melba is a small Canyon County farming town about 12 miles south of Nampa and three miles from the Snake River, founded in 1912 and home to 572 people at the 2020 census. It calls itself the Seed Heart of America for the seed crops grown on the surrounding farms.

Plumbing here is rural plumbing: private wells, pressure tanks, septic systems, long buried lines to shops and barns, and freeze risk on anything outside the heated house. Emergency calls from the town and the farm roads around it are answered at any hour.

Wells and Pressure Tanks

When the Well Pump Will Not Stop Cycling

Without a city meter to watch, a Melba household usually learns about a leak from the well system itself. A pump that kicks on when nobody is using water, or cycles on and off every few seconds, points to a leak somewhere downstream or a waterlogged pressure tank.

Both wear out the pump and pressure switch quickly. We isolate the house, outbuilding and yard lines one at a time while watching the pressure gauge, the same approach our leak detection in Nampa uses, and check the tank's air charge.

Private well water also varies more than city water. A lab test before buying treatment equipment tells you whether you need a softener, iron treatment or filtration, or nothing at all.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Melba

On a well property the pump keeps pushing water into a broken line until it is switched off, so the first step here is different from a city home.

  • 1Shut off the main valve or the well pump. Switch off the well pump breaker at the panel or well house, then close the main valve at the house.
  • 2Kill power to wet areas. In shops and barns, water and power cords often share the floor. Cut the breaker before stepping near either.
  • 3Clear the immediate area. Move feed, tools and stored items off the floor, and keep livestock away from flooded ground.
  • 4Call our 24/7 line. Give the road name and nearest cross street, since many farm addresses are hard to find at night.
Winter on the Farm

Frozen Lines in Shops, Barns and Well Houses

The house is rarely the first thing to freeze on a Melba property. Unheated shops, barns, well houses and livestock water lines sit in cold air all winter, and buried lines that rise out of the ground at a hydrant or spigot freeze at that exposed section.

A line that froze and split in January often is not discovered until spring, when water goes back on and a puddle appears in the shop. Walking every outbuilding line on first turn-on catches those before they waste well water or flood a floor.

If a line has frozen, our frozen pipe repair service thaws it without open flame and checks for splits.

Septic Systems and What Goes Down the Drain

Most Melba-area properties outside the small town core rely on septic systems. Grease, wipes and heavy garbage disposal use add solids to the tank and can back up household drains long before the tank itself is due for pumping.

When drains back up on a septic property, the question is whether the clog is in the house line or the tank is full. A plumber clears and cameras the line, and a septic service pumps the tank, so knowing which one to call first saves a trip. Melba also sits near Celebration Park and the Snake River Birds of Prey area, so protecting groundwater with a healthy septic system matters here.
