University District around NNU

Plumber in the University District, Nampa, ID

Nampa's University District is the roughly one-square-mile area around Northwest Nazarene University. The city's 2009 University District Plan describes it as bounded by East Roosevelt Avenue, South Powerline Road, East Hawaii Avenue and 12th Avenue.

Homes here were built in waves, and the plan notes that owner occupancy runs below the city average. That mix of older houses and rentals shapes the plumbing calls that come from these streets.

Where It Is

The University District on the Map

The 2009 plan draws the district between East Roosevelt Avenue on the north, South Powerline Road on the east, East Hawaii Avenue on the south and 12th Avenue on the west. Streets named in the plan include Holly Street, East Dewey Avenue, East Amity Avenue and Garrity Boulevard.

Northwest Nazarene University, founded in 1913, sits at the center. The H. Orton Wiley House at 524 East Dewey Avenue has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1986.

The Housing

Three Building Eras on the Same Streets

The city's plan describes major construction in the 1940s, 1970s and 1990s. The earliest homes were built for returning World War II veterans and typically have two bedrooms, wood siding and detached garages that open onto alleys.

Because the eras sit side by side, plumbing can differ from one house to the next on the same block. A 1940s house may have had its pipes replaced already, while a 1990s house may still have its original water heater and fixtures. Looking at exposed pipe and the date on the water heater label is the quickest way to tell.

The plan also notes that two-bedroom homes are the most common type here and that owner occupancy is lower than the city's. Rentals change hands often, which is when small plumbing problems tend to surface.

For Landlords and Tenants

Turnover Safety Steps for University District Rentals

A quick check between tenants catches small problems before the next household moves in.

  • 1Run every fixture. Look under each sink while water runs and check that every drain clears quickly.
  • 2Listen to the toilets. A toilet that refills on its own is leaking from the tank. A worn flapper or fill valve is a small part to replace.
  • 3Read the water heater label. Note the manufacture date and look for rust or moisture at the base.
  • 4Show the new tenant the shutoff. Point out the main valve and the valves under each sink so a leak can be stopped quickly.

Cost and Safety

What Shapes a Repair Bill in a University District Home

The cost of a repair here depends less on the street than on the house: how easy the pipe is to reach through the crawlspace, what the pipe is made of, how many fixtures are involved and whether the call comes after hours. Ask for the price before any work starts so there are no surprises.

One safety point matters in every rental. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires a temperature and pressure relief valve on every storage water heater. If that valve drips, do not cap or plug it. Call for a repair instead, because a blocked relief valve is dangerous.
