Sewer Scope and Line Inspection

Sewer Camera Inspection in Nampa, ID

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your sewer line so you can see its condition on a screen: roots, cracks, sags, offset joints, grease buildup or a clean healthy pipe. In Nampa, Idaho, owners usually book one before buying a house, after the second or third main line clog, or before a remodel adds a bathroom.

You watch the footage as it runs, you get a recording, and the camera's locator tells us how deep and where any problem sits.

Sound Familiar?

Clogs That Keep Coming Back, or a House You Are About to Buy

People rarely ask for a camera by name. They say the main line has been snaked twice this year, or the toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or the inspector on a home purchase suggested a sewer scope. All three are good reasons to look inside the pipe.

Home-buying advisories for Nampa homes suggest a sewer camera inspection on houses more than about 20 years old. A standard home inspection does not look inside the sewer line, and a damaged line discovered after closing is the new owner's cost.

The camera answers one question clearly: is this line dirty, or is it damaged? Dirty lines need cleaning. Damaged lines need repair, and cleaning them repeatedly only delays that.

How It Works

What Happens During an Inspection

The camera goes in through a cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation. If there is no accessible cleanout, a toilet can be pulled to reach the line, though adding a cleanout is often worth doing for future service. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for accessible cleanouts on building drains for this reason.

As the camera travels, a transmitter in its head lets us trace its position and depth from the surface. When we find a problem, we mark its spot on the ground, which matters a great deal if a repair means digging.

If the line is fully blocked, the camera cannot pass. In that case we clear it with a main line cable first, then inspect.

Newer Homes Benefit Too

A young sewer line is less likely to have root or corrosion damage, but it is not immune to problems. Construction debris, a joint that shifted as fill soil settled, or a sag from poor bedding can all show up in the first few years.

In fast-growing towns near Nampa such as Star, where most housing is recent, a camera look during the builder warranty period can document a defect while someone else is still responsible for fixing it.
