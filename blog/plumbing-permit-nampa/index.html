Do You Need a Plumbing Permit in Nampa? City of Nampa vs. Idaho DOPL

Written by the Nampa Express Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026

Permits are the least exciting part of any plumbing job and one of the easiest to get wrong. Two things trip people up in Nampa: which office issues the permit, and which jobs need one at all.

This guide explains both, based on the City of Nampa's building department rules and Idaho's state licensing agency. It is general guidance. Always confirm with the office that has jurisdiction over your address.

The short version: inside Nampa city limits, most work that adds, moves or replaces piping needs a city plumbing permit, while simple repairs and like-for-like fixture swaps do not. Outside the city, the state often issues permits instead. The sections below explain the details and the one homeowner exception that surprises people.

Who Issues It

City Permit or State Permit

The deciding factor is jurisdiction, not ZIP code.

Other incorporated citiesThat city, or the state if the city does not issue its ownVaries by city

DOPL now handles the licensing and permitting work formerly done by Idaho's Division of Building Safety. DOPL also notes that a local building permit does not always replace the need for a separate plumbing permit.

Which Jobs

What Needs a Permit Inside Nampa

According to the City of Nampa's plumbing permit rules, the requirement is broad: plumbing piping and drainage work requires a permit from the Building Department, with exceptions for repairs to leaks, replacing broken fixtures and similar emergency repairs.

In practice, that means new supply or drain piping, relocating fixtures, water heater installations, sewer and water service line work, and gas piping generally need a permit. The city's residential guidance specifically says that moving sinks, tubs, showers or toilets requires one.

Replacing a faucet, fixing a leak or swapping a toilet in the same spot generally does not.

Who Can Do It

Licensed Plumbers and the Homeowner Exception

Nampa requires permitted plumbing to be installed by a state-licensed journeyman plumber employed by an Idaho-licensed plumbing contractor. The contractor normally pulls the permit and schedules inspection.

There is a homeowner exception: owners of a single-family home they occupy may do their own permitted plumbing under certain conditions, including living in the home for a period afterward. The work still needs a permit and inspection.

Idaho DOPL offers an online license search, so you can confirm any contractor's license is active before hiring.

Why Skipping the Permit Costs More Later

The City of Nampa warns that non-permitted work can delay the sale of a home, require permits after the fact and require bringing the work up to code, and that removal of work may be required at the owner's cost. Buyers' inspectors and appraisers increasingly ask about recent plumbing work.

A permitted water heater replacement or repipe leaves a record that the work was inspected, which helps at resale.

Hiring

What to Do Before Permitted Work Starts

  • 1Confirm your jurisdiction. Inside Nampa city limits or not? Check your property tax records or ask the city.
  • 2Ask who pulls the permit. The contractor should. Be cautious if you are asked to pull it yourself.
  • 3Check the license. Use DOPL's license search to confirm the contractor is active.
  • 4Plan for inspection access. Inspectors need to see work before walls or trenches are closed.
  • 5Keep the final inspection record. File it with your home documents.
Outside Nampa

Neighboring Towns

Nearby cities set their own processes. In Kuna, Caldwell, Middleton, Star and Melba, the plumber confirms which office applies to the address and handles the permit as part of the job.

Inspection

What Happens at a Plumbing Inspection

For most permitted jobs, an inspector checks the work before it is covered, for example new piping before drywall goes up or a sewer line before the trench is backfilled. New water piping is typically pressure-tested so the inspector can confirm it holds.

For a water heater replacement, the inspection usually covers the venting, gas connection, relief valve discharge and any required expansion tank. If something fails, the contractor corrects it and the inspector returns.

Scheduling inspections is part of the contractor's job, but the homeowner may need to provide access. Ask the contractor when inspections are expected so someone can be home.

Selling a Home

Permits and Your Home Sale

When you sell, buyers and their inspectors may ask about recent plumbing work such as a new water heater, a repipe or a remodeled bathroom. Being able to show permits and passed inspections answers that question quickly.

Unpermitted work does not always stop a sale, but it can lead to repair requests, lower offers or delays while it is permitted after the fact. The City of Nampa has warned that discovery of non-permitted work may delay a sale and require bringing work up to code.

Keeping a simple folder of permits, invoices and inspection records for every plumbing project makes the eventual sale smoother.
