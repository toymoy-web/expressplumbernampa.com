Water First

How Water Reaches a Kuna Home

Most Kuna subdivisions run two systems: city drinking water and pressurized irrigation through the Kuna Municipal Irrigation District. The city lists irrigation pressure at roughly 52 to 58 psi with 7.5 to 15 gallons per minute, depending on location, which is useful to know when a sprinkler zone suddenly underperforms.

Not every neighborhood got irrigation lines when it was built. City records from 2013 describe subdivisions such as Butler, Oasis and Plateau that were annexed into the irrigation district without pressurized lines, leaving owners to water lawns with drinking water. If your home is in that situation, a leak in an outdoor line shows up directly on the water bill.

Kuna's code requires new subdivisions to install pressurized irrigation and prohibits cross-connections between domestic and irrigation lines that do not meet city rules, in line with the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That is where backflow devices and their yearly tests come in.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Kuna

Most Kuna homes are recent builds, where the main shutoff is usually in the garage near the water heater or at the front wall. Know which valve is yours before you need it.

  • 1Shut off the main valve. Turn the house main in the garage or utility area. If the water is coming from a sprinkler line, close your own irrigation valve and leave the district's valve alone.
  • 2Kill power to wet areas. Switch off breakers for any room with standing water, reaching the panel from dry floor only.
  • 3Clear the immediate area. Move furniture, rugs and anything plugged in away from the water, and keep kids out of that room.
  • 4Call our 24/7 line. Say whether it is a drinking water line or an irrigation line, which changes who fixes it and how fast.

Winter

Freeze Risk on Kuna's Open Ground

Kuna sits on open high-desert ground south of the Boise River valley, next to the Snake River Plain. Winter nights regularly drop well below freezing, and exposed lines in crawlspaces, hose bibs and irrigation assemblies are the first to split.

Draining the irrigation system through your own valve after the season, disconnecting hoses and insulating crawlspace lines prevents most January calls. If a line has already frozen, our pipe thawing service thaws and checks it.

Homes just over the line in southwest Boise's 83709 ZIP code sit a short drive from Kuna. We cover them for the two calls that dominate winter here: burst supply lines, and getting hot water back when a tank quits in the cold.
