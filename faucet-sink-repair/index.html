Faucets, Sinks and Hose Bibs

Faucet & Sink Repair in Nampa, ID

Faucet and sink repair in Nampa, Idaho covers the fixtures you use most: kitchen and bathroom faucets that drip or wobble, sinks that leak underneath, shutoff valves that no longer close, and outdoor hose bibs that split in the first hard freeze.

Most of these are small jobs when caught early. The costly ones are slow leaks inside a sink cabinet that soak the cabinet floor for months.

Hard Water

Why Faucet Cartridges Fail Early in Some Nampa Homes

The City of Nampa reports well water hardness from none to about 7 grains per gallon. On the harder end, minerals crust inside aerators and around cartridge seals, which leads to low flow, stiff handles and drips within a few years.

Soaking an aerator in vinegar clears light scale. A cartridge that drips or sticks is usually cheaper to replace than to clean, and matching the brand and model keeps the faucet working as designed. If scale shows up on every fixture, softening the supply addresses the cause.

Scaled aerator: soak in vinegar, rinse, refit.
Dripping cartridge: replace with the matching part.
Scale on every fixture: test your hardness.

Outdoor Faucets

Hose Bibs, Freeze Damage and Frost-Free Sillcocks

Outdoor spigots are the most common freeze casualty in Nampa, where the city lists an average January low of 21.1°F. A standard hose bib holds water right at the outside wall, and that water freezes and splits the valve or the pipe behind it.

A frost-free sillcock moves the shutoff inside the heated wall, so the section outside drains when you close it. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because an attached hose traps water in the valve. A hose left on over winter is a common reason frost-free units still split. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) also requires backflow protection at hose connections, which most new sillcocks build in as a vacuum breaker.

If a hose bib froze and the pipe behind it split inside the wall, you may not see the leak until spring when you turn it on. Our guide to fixing frozen and split lines covers that situation.

Before You Call

What to Do When a Faucet or Sink Is Leaking

  • 1Close the fixture shutoffs. Most sinks have two small valves underneath. Turn them clockwise. If they do not close, use the main.
  • 2Dry the cabinet. Empty it and dry the floor so you can see where new water appears.
  • 3Check the supply lines. Braided lines with bulges or rust at the ends are near failure.
  • 4Note the faucet brand. The name on the handle or base helps the plumber bring the right cartridge.
  • 5Disconnect outdoor hoses in fall. A few seconds of work that prevents most hose bib freeze damage.
