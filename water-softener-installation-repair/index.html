Water Softeners

Water Softener Installation & Repair in Nampa, ID

A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium that cause scale, using resin beads that trade those minerals for sodium or potassium. Whether a Nampa, Idaho home needs one depends on which city well serves it, because the city reports hardness from none up to about 7 grains per gallon.

We test your water first, size the unit to your actual hardness and household use, install it with a bypass valve and a proper drain, and repair existing softeners that have stopped regenerating.

Do You Need One?

What Your Hardness Reading Means

The Water Quality Association classifies water hardness on a simple scale. Here is how it translates into a decision for a Nampa home.

Hardness (grains per gallon)ClassificationWhat to expect
0 to 3.5SoftA softener adds little. Skip it unless on a well.
3.5 to 7Moderately hardVisible scale on fixtures, shorter water heater life. A softener pays off over time.
7 to 10.5HardHeavy scale, spotty dishes, softener recommended
Over 10.5Very hardCommon on some private wells. Softener plus possible iron treatment.

Most of Nampa's city water falls in the first two rows, which is why testing your own tap matters more than a general answer. Homes on private wells outside city limits can test much higher and often carry iron as well.

Softener or Conditioner

Salt-Based Softeners Versus Salt-Free Conditioners

A salt-based softener actually removes hardness minerals. It needs salt, a drain for the regeneration cycle and electricity for the control valve, and it is the only type that makes water measurably soft.

A salt-free conditioner changes how minerals behave so they are less likely to stick as scale, but it does not remove them. Water still tests hard. It needs no salt or drain, which suits some homes, but be wary of anyone selling it as a softener.

For appliance protection, a real softener does the most for a tank or tankless water heater, whose heat exchangers suffer first from scale.

Installation

What a Correct Softener Install Includes

The softener goes on the main line after the point where outdoor hose bibs branch off, so you are not softening water for the lawn. In Nampa homes on pressurized irrigation, that issue is smaller, but hose bibs still usually stay on hard water.

A bypass valve lets you shut the softener out of the system for service. The regeneration drain line needs an air gap so waste water can never be siphoned back into the supply, a requirement under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Many owners also keep one cold tap at the kitchen sink on unsoftened water for drinking and cooking.

Sizing matters more than brand. A unit too small regenerates constantly and wastes salt. A unit too large can sit so long between cycles that the resin performs poorly. We size by your measured hardness, the number of people in the house and daily water use.

Troubleshooting

What to Check Before You Call About a Softener

  • 1Salt level. The brine tank should have salt above the water line. An empty tank means no regeneration.
  • 2Salt bridge. A hard crust can form across the tank with an empty space underneath. Push a broom handle down gently to break it.
  • 3Power and clock. A power outage can reset the timer so the unit regenerates at the wrong time or not at all.
  • 4Bypass position. Make sure the bypass valve was not left in bypass after other plumbing work.
  • 5Water on the floor. Check the drain line and brine tank overflow before assuming the valve has failed.
Pricing

What Softener Installation and Repair Cost Depends On

The unit's capacity and control valve set most of the price. Installation cost depends on how easy it is to get the main line, a drain and power to the same spot.
