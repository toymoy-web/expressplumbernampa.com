What Brand-New Middleton Homes Still Need

Recent communities such as The Mill at Middleton are built on crawl space foundations with gas water heaters, city water and sewer, and pressurized irrigation. That is a solid setup, but a crawlspace means supply lines sit in a space that can reach freezing temperatures in winter.

Insulated crawlspace lines, closed foundation vents in cold snaps and frost-free hose bibs with hoses disconnected prevent most winter damage. If a line does freeze, our crew can thaw a frozen line safely and checks for splits.

New homes also benefit from a sewer camera inspection before the builder warranty ends, since a sag or construction debris in a young line is easier to get fixed while someone else is still responsible.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Middleton

In the crawl space homes that make up most of Middleton's newer housing, a split line often floods the space under the floor before anyone sees water indoors.

  • 1Shut off the main valve. Look near the crawlspace hatch or on the garage wall, and turn it fully clockwise.
  • 2Kill power to wet areas. If water is pooling near outlets or under the house near wiring, cut the breaker from a dry spot and stay out of the crawlspace.
  • 3Clear the immediate area. Pull rugs and stored items away from any wet floor, and prop the crawlspace hatch open only if it is safe to reach.
  • 4Call our 24/7 line. Mention if the leak is under the house, so the crew brings crawlspace lighting and pumps.

Before Winter

What to Do Before the First Hard Freeze in a Middleton Home

Most Middleton homes built in the last two decades sit over crawl spaces, so fall prep is mostly about the space under the house.

  • 1Check crawlspace insulation. Look for supply lines with missing or fallen pipe insulation, especially near foundation vents and the access hatch.
  • 2Close foundation vents in cold snaps. If your home's design allows it, closing vents during hard freezes keeps crawlspace air warmer.
  • 3Drain irrigation through your own valve. Once the season ends, drain or blow out your sprinkler lines and any backflow assembly.
  • 4Disconnect every hose. A frost-free sillcock only works with the hose removed, and its built-in vacuum breaker, a Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirement for hose connections, can freeze too.
  • 5Know your shutoff. Find the main valve before you need it, and make sure it closes fully.
