Downtown Nampa

Plumber in Downtown Nampa, ID

Downtown Nampa mixes long-standing storefronts, restaurants, offices and upstairs apartments around the historic depot. The city's form-based code covers the downtown area between 10th and 16th Avenues South.

Plumbing here is part commercial and part residential, often in the same building. Older storefronts, kitchen grease lines and backflow assemblies sit next to the apartments and homes on the surrounding blocks.

Where It Is

Downtown Nampa on the Map

The City of Nampa's form-based code applies to the downtown area bounded by 10th Avenue and 16th Avenue South. At its core, the Nampa Historic District covers the 1200 and 1300 blocks of 1st Street South and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1983.

The Nampa Depot, opened in 1903 for the Oregon Short Line Railroad, has housed the Canyon County Historical Society's Nampa headquarters since 1973. The Old Nampa Neighborhood historic district begins a few blocks to the south.

The Buildings

Older Storefronts and Mixed-Use Plumbing

According to the city, the Nampa Historic District is made up of ten commercial buildings with a period of significance from 1905 to 1920. Individually listed buildings nearby include the Farmers and Merchants Bank at 101 11th Avenue South and the U.S. Post Office building at 123 11th Avenue South.

Buildings of this age have usually been remodeled many times, with new tenants adding restrooms, kitchens and sinks over the decades. When a space changes hands, it is worth checking that the drain lines and water service can handle the new use before the build-out starts.

Upstairs apartments add another layer. A leak in an upper unit can reach the business below, so knowing where each unit's shutoff is matters in a shared building.

Shared Buildings

One Building, Several Tenants, One Water Service

The city's listing for the Nampa Historic District includes the Dewey Building and the Blue Eye Building, both from 1909, and two Nampa Department Store buildings. Buildings like these often share one water service and one building drain among several tenants.

That changes how a repair goes. Shutting off water for one shop can affect the restaurant next door or the apartments upstairs, so it helps to know which valve feeds which space and to give neighbors notice before planned work. For a leak, the closest valve to the problem is the one to close first.

Cost and Code

What Affects Plumbing Costs in a Downtown Building

Downtown jobs are priced by access and disruption as much as by parts. Work that would shut water off for several tenants is often scheduled early or after closing, and reaching a shared building drain can mean working through a basement or a neighboring space. Ask for the price before work starts.

Food businesses have one extra cost to plan for. Nampa's wastewater pretreatment program requires grease interceptors, sized to Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) tables, at food service businesses, so a new restaurant build-out should budget for one from the start.

For Building Owners

Before a Downtown Remodel or New Lease

A few checks before work starts save surprises later in an older downtown building.

  • 1Map every shutoff. Label the main and each unit's valve, and make sure tenants know where theirs is.
  • 2Scope the building drain. A camera look at the line to the street shows whether it can take a new kitchen or restroom.
  • 3Plan for grease. Food businesses need a grease interceptor sized for the kitchen, and the lines that feed it need regular cleaning.
  • 4Check the backflow assembly. Idaho's drinking water rules call for annual testing of installed backflow assemblies.
