Which Parts of a Treasure Valley Home Freeze First

Written by the Nampa Express Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026

Nampa does not get Minnesota winters, which is exactly why frozen pipes catch people off guard. The City of Nampa lists an average January low of about 21°F, and most winters bring a stretch of nights in the teens. That is plenty cold enough to freeze water in a pipe that sits against outside air.

The good news is that frozen pipes are predictable. They happen in the same few places in almost every Treasure Valley house. Protect those spots and you remove most of the risk.

Ranked

The Usual Freeze Points, Most Likely First

This ranking reflects how exposed each location is to outside air, which is what actually freezes water.

RankLocationWhy it freezes
1Hose bibs and outdoor spigotsWater sits right at the exterior wall, often with a hose still attached
2Crawlspace supply linesFoundation vents let cold air circulate around uninsulated pipe
3Pipes in exterior wallsLittle insulation between the pipe and the siding, especially on north walls
4Garage lines and water heatersUnheated space, worse when the door is left open
5Irrigation and backflow assembliesWater left in after the October shutdown
Number One

Hose Bibs: Small Valve, Big Leak

A standard outdoor faucet holds water right at the wall. When it freezes, the valve body or the pipe just inside splits. You often will not know until spring, when you open the spigot and water pours inside the wall instead of out of the hose.

Frost-free sillcocks move the actual shutoff several inches inside the heated wall, so the exposed part drains when closed. They only work if the hose is disconnected, because a hose traps water in the valve. A foam cover adds a little protection, but disconnecting the hose matters far more.

Number Two

Crawlspaces: Where Most Valley Homes Hide Their Pipes

Many Treasure Valley houses sit over vented crawlspaces rather than basements or slabs. Those vents exist to control moisture, but in a cold snap they pull freezing air right across the supply lines.

Check that every supply pipe in the crawlspace wears insulation, with no gaps at elbows and valves. Pay extra attention near the access hatch and along the side facing prevailing winds. If your home's design allows it, closing foundation vents during hard freezes keeps the crawlspace noticeably warmer.

Crawlspace lines are also where many leaks start. A Nampa leak detection visit can check a crawlspace that feels damp or smells musty after winter.

Insulate every supply line, including elbows.
Close vents in hard freezes if the design allows.
Check near the hatch and windward side.
Numbers Three to Five

Exterior Walls, Garages and Irrigation

Kitchen and bathroom sinks on exterior walls freeze when cabinet doors trap cold air around the pipes. Opening those doors on the coldest nights lets house heat reach them, and a slow drip keeps water moving.

Garages are unheated by design. Water heaters and laundry lines there are safe while the door stays closed and the house wall stays warm. Leaving the garage open on a cold night is how a laundry supply line splits.

Irrigation lines and backflow assemblies freeze when they are not drained after the season ends in early October. That damage shows up in spring rather than winter, but it starts on the first hard freeze.

Thawing Safely

What to Do If a Pipe Freezes

  • 1Keep the faucet open. Moving meltwater helps the rest thaw and relieves pressure.
  • 2Use gentle heat. A hair dryer, a space heater kept at a safe distance or warm towels. Never a torch or open flame.
  • 3Work from the faucet back. Start near the open tap so meltwater has an exit.
  • 4Watch for leaks as it thaws. Splits often only show once pressure returns. Have the main shutoff ready.
  • 5Call if you cannot reach it. Pipes inside finished walls are safer thawed by a plumber with proper equipment. See frozen pipe repair in Nampa.

Before Next Winter

A one-time fall walk-through handles most of this: insulate crawlspace lines, swap old hose bibs for frost-free sillcocks, disconnect hoses, drain irrigation through your own valve and know where the main shutoff is.

Newer homes are not exempt. Many recent builds in towns like Middleton sit over crawl spaces too, so the same checklist applies on day one.

Going Away

Leaving Home During a Cold Snap

Many frozen pipe calls come from homes whose owners were away over the holidays. Keep the heat on while you are gone, set to a steady temperature rather than turned way down, and leave cabinet doors open on exterior-wall sinks.

For a longer trip, shutting off the main water valve and opening a faucet at the lowest point drains much of the system. If a pipe does split while you are away, far less water escapes.

Ask a neighbor to check the house after a hard freeze. A leak found in a day does a fraction of the damage of one found in a week.

New Construction

Why Newer Homes Still Freeze

Modern building codes and PEX piping have made freezing less common, but not rare. Newer homes can still have supply lines routed near exterior walls, crawlspaces with vents left open all winter, and hose bibs with hoses left attached.

PEX tolerates freezing better than copper because it can stretch slightly, but its fittings can still pull apart or crack. A frozen PEX line is also still a line with no water.

If you own a newer home, spend ten minutes the first fall finding the main shutoff, checking crawlspace insulation and disconnecting hoses. Those three steps cover most of the risk regardless of the home's age.
