Sewer Line Replacement in Nampa, ID

Sewer line replacement means installing a new pipe for the whole run from your house to the connection with the public sewer, rather than fixing one damaged spot. In Nampa, Idaho, it is the right call when a camera shows damage along most of the line, when the old pipe has lost its shape, or when repeated spot repairs keep chasing new failures.

We confirm the need with a camera first, compare trenchless and open-trench methods for your yard, and handle the permit and inspection.

Repair or Replace

When a Spot Repair Stops Making Sense

One cracked joint or one root-filled section is a spot repair job. Replacement becomes the better value when the damage is spread out: several broken sections, heavy root intrusion at many joints, a line that has flattened or collapsed, or a long sag that holds water.

Cost per foot drops when the whole line is done at once, because the crew mobilizes once and the surface is opened once. Paying for three separate spot repairs over five years usually costs more than one replacement, and it leaves old pipe in the ground between the patches.

Methods

Pipe Bursting, Lining and Open Trench Compared

Three methods can replace or renew a full sewer line. Which one fits depends on the old pipe's condition, depth and what sits on top of it.

MethodHow it worksBest whenLimits
Pipe burstingA bursting head pulls new pipe through the old line, splitting the old pipe outwardOld pipe is broken but the path is straightNeeds pits at each end
Cured-in-place liningA resin liner is cured inside the old pipe, forming a new pipe within itOld pipe still holds its shapeCannot fix a sag or collapse
Open trenchThe line is dug up and replaced with new pipeSags, collapses, shallow lines under lawnSurface restoration needed

Trenchless methods avoid a long trench across the yard or driveway. They are not always cheaper, and they cannot correct grade, so a line with a belly still needs digging at that spot.

Nampa Rules

Permits, Excavation and Inspection in Nampa

Sewer line replacement is permitted plumbing work. Inside Nampa city limits, the City of Nampa Building Department issues the plumbing permit, and the new line is inspected before it is buried.

The city also sets qualifications for excavation contractors who install private sewer and water service lines outside the building envelope, so the digging side of the job has its own requirements. Before any excavation, the underground utility locate through Idaho's 811 system marks gas, power and communication lines.

New sewer piping is installed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho's plumbing code is based on, including proper slope and accessible cleanouts. A new cleanout near the foundation is worth adding if the old line never had one, since it makes every future inspection and cleaning easier.

Before Work Starts

What to Do to Prepare for a Sewer Replacement

  • 1Plan for limited water use. During the tie-in, drains cannot be used. Plan showers, laundry and dishes around that window.
  • 2Mark private lines. The 811 locate covers utility lines, not private sprinkler lines, low-voltage lighting or buried pet fences. Tell the crew where yours run.
  • 3Clear the access area. Move vehicles off the driveway if the line runs under it, and clear planters or stored items near the cleanout.
  • 4Ask about restoration. Confirm who restores lawn, landscaping or concrete over the trench or pits.
  • 5Keep the camera footage. Before and after recordings document the work for future buyers.
