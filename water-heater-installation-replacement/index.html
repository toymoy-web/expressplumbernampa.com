Repair or Replace

Where the Ten-Year Line Actually Falls

Age alone does not condemn a tank. A seven-year-old gas unit with a failed thermocouple is a quick fix, and so is a burnt-out element in an electric heater. Those jobs belong on our water heater repair page, and we will tell you when a repair makes more sense.

Replacement wins when water weeps from the tank shell itself, when the tank is past about ten years and needs a costly part, or when you are paying for repeated service calls. A corroded shell cannot be patched, and a tank near the end of its life rarely justifies a new gas valve.

Local Water

Why Some Nampa Tanks Wear Out Faster Than Others

Nampa draws all of its drinking water from municipal groundwater wells, and the city's water quality report lists hardness anywhere from none to about 7 grains per gallon. Which well supplies your street makes a real difference to a water heater.

At the harder end of that range, calcium settles on the bottom of a gas tank as a crust. The burner then heats through a layer of mineral, which wastes gas and makes the popping or rumbling sound many owners notice after year five. In electric tanks the same scale coats the lower element until it burns out.

The practical takeaway: if your faucet aerators crust over quickly, plan on flushing a new tank once a year and checking the anode rod every few years. A softener for hard water is worth pricing at the same time as the heater if hardness tests high, since it protects the new tank from day one.

Code and Safety

What a Proper Replacement Includes

A cheap install skips the parts that keep a water heater safe for the next decade. Every replacement we quote covers these items where your home needs them.

  • 1Permit. Water heater replacement is permitted plumbing work in Idaho. Inside Nampa city limits the permit comes from the City of Nampa Building Department, and outside the city it usually runs through Idaho DOPL.
  • 2Thermal expansion tank. Homes with a pressure-reducing valve or a backflow device have a closed system, so the heated water has nowhere to expand. A small expansion tank keeps that pressure off the relief valve and your fixtures.
  • 3Relief valve discharge line. The temperature and pressure relief valve gets a full-size discharge pipe routed to a safe, visible termination point.
  • 4Venting and gas connection. Gas units get a draft check at the vent and a sediment trap on the gas supply, following the Uniform Plumbing Code that Idaho's plumbing code is built on.
  • 5Drain pan where needed. Heaters installed over finished space get a pan piped to a drain so a future leak does not reach the ceiling below.
  • 6Haul-away. The old tank leaves with us, drained and disconnected.

Efficiency Options

Heat Pump Units and Current Idaho Incentives

A heat pump water heater pulls warmth from the surrounding air, so it needs open space around it and works best in a garage or a large utility room. It also cools and dries that space slightly, which most Treasure Valley owners do not mind in July and do notice in January, when the unit leans more on its backup electric element.

Idaho Power runs an incentive for heat pump water heaters installed in existing homes, limited to one per home and paid after you submit the paid invoice. Intermountain Gas has offered rebates on tankless units rated 0.91 UEF or higher. The federal 25C tax credit for these upgrades ended on December 31, 2025, so utility programs are now the main incentive. Check each program's current terms before you buy, because eligibility rules change.

Heat pump units: Idaho Power incentive, one per existing home.
Tankless gas units: Intermountain Gas rebates have required 0.91 UEF or better.
Federal 25C credit: ended December 31, 2025.
Sizing

Picking the Right Tank Size for Your Household

An undersized tank runs out of hot water every morning, and an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra water hot. These are common starting points, adjusted for how your household actually uses hot water.

HouseholdTypical tank sizeWhen to size up
1 to 2 people30 to 40 gallonsLarge soaking tub or frequent laundry
3 to 4 people40 to 50 gallonsBack-to-back morning showers
5 or more people50 to 75 gallons, or tanklessTeenagers, multiple bathrooms in use at once

First-hour rating, printed on the unit's yellow EnergyGuide label, matters more than tank size alone. It tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour, and it is the number to compare between models.

Install Day

What to Do Before the Installer Arrives

  • 1Clear a path. The old tank leaves and the new one comes in through the garage or the nearest door. Move cars and stored boxes out of the way.
  • 2Plan for a few hours without hot water. Run the dishwasher and laundry the day before.
  • 3Find the gas or breaker shutoff. Knowing where the water heater's gas valve or electric breaker is speeds up the disconnect.
  • 4Check the space around the tank. Gas units need clearance for combustion air. Items stacked against the heater have to move.
  • 5Keep the paperwork. The permit card, model and serial number and the install date all matter for warranty claims later.
