Shower & Bathtub Installation in Nampa, ID

Shower and bathtub installation in Nampa, Idaho covers the plumbing side of a bathroom update: replacing a worn tub, converting a tub to a walk-in shower, installing a new shower valve, and relocating drains when the layout changes.

The finish is what people see. The valve, drain and waterproofing behind it decide whether the new shower lasts twenty years or leaks into the subfloor in two. That hidden work is where we focus.

The Valve

Why the Shower Valve Matters Most

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires showers to have pressure-balancing or thermostatic valves, which keep water from suddenly scalding someone when a toilet flushes elsewhere in the house. Older two-handle valves often do not have that protection.

A remodel is the cheapest time to upgrade, because the wall is already open. The valve also sets the height and location of every control, so it has to be placed before tile goes up.

On harder Nampa water, cartridges in shower valves scale up over time. A valve brand with readily available cartridges makes future repairs simple. Pairing a new valve with softened water protects it.

Tub to Shower

Converting a Tub to a Walk-In Shower

Tub-to-shower conversions are one of the most common bathroom changes, often for easier access as owners age. The tub drain usually sits at one end and at a different depth than a shower drain needs, so the drain is relocated and a proper trap installed.

Waterproofing is the other critical piece. A shower pan or waterproof tray under the tile, with membrane up the walls, keeps water out of the framing. Grout and tile are not waterproof on their own.

In homes over a crawlspace, which describes much of the Treasure Valley, drain relocation is usually easier because the plumber can work from below. Slab homes need the concrete opened at the new drain location.

Planning

What to Do Before a Shower or Tub Project

  • 1Decide on the layout first. Moving the drain or valve changes the plumbing scope and the permit.
  • 2Choose the valve before the tile. The valve sets control heights and needs to be in the wall before finish work.
  • 3Plan access. A crawlspace or ceiling below makes drain work easier. Note what is under the bathroom.
  • 4Check the water heater. A large soaking tub may need more hot water than an older tank delivers. See water heater options.
  • 5Ask who does the finish work. Confirm who handles tile and drywall so the plumbing and finish schedules line up.
