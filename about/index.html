Our Standards

What You Can Expect on Every Job

Idaho licenses plumbers through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL), and permitted plumbing work in Nampa must be done by a licensed journeyman working for a licensed contractor. Work is done to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho's plumbing code is based on, and permits are pulled where the job requires them.

Every page on this site is written around real Nampa conditions: the city's well water and its hardness range, the separate pressurized irrigation system many homes have, winter freeze risk and the local permit rules. The plumbing services page links to each one.

Check for Yourself

What to Do to Verify Any Plumber in Idaho

Whoever you hire, these checks take a few minutes and protect you.

  • 1Look up the license. Idaho DOPL has an online license search. Ask for the contractor's license number and check that it is active.
  • 2Ask who pulls the permit. For permitted work, the contractor should pull it. Be cautious if you are asked to pull it yourself.
  • 3Get the price before work starts. A written or clearly stated price before the repair is standard practice.
  • 4Ask about warranty terms. Labor and parts warranties vary by contractor. Ask before the job, not after.
  • 5Keep the paperwork. Invoices describing the cause and repair help with insurance, resale and future service.

Local Conditions

Why Nampa Plumbing Has Its Own Quirks

Three local facts shape most plumbing work here. The city's drinking water comes entirely from groundwater wells, with hardness ranging from none to about 7 grains per gallon depending on the well, which drives scale in water heaters and fixtures.

Many subdivisions also have a separate canal-fed pressurized irrigation system, so a wet yard can come from either of two buried lines. Knowing which one is leaking changes who fixes it.

And winters bring an average January low around 21°F, cold enough to split pipes in crawlspaces, exterior walls and hose bibs. Each of these gets a dedicated page, such as our frozen pipe page, written for these conditions.
