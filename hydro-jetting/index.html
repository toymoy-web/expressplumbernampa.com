High-Pressure Line Cleaning

Hydro Jetting in Nampa, ID

Hydro jetting cleans drain and sewer pipes with a stream of high-pressure water from a specialized nozzle that scrubs the full inside wall of the pipe. In Nampa, Idaho, it is the next step when a line has been snaked and clogs again, when grease has built up in a kitchen line, or when roots keep returning to a main sewer.

Where a cable machine punches a hole through a blockage, jetting removes the buildup around it. That is why jetted lines tend to stay clear longer.

Sound Familiar?

Snaked It Twice and It Clogged Again

Most people arrive at hydro jetting after something else did not work. The line was snaked last month and it is slow again. A cable got stuck partway down the main. The kitchen sink backs up every few weeks no matter what goes down it. Or the plumber who cleared it mentioned roots.

Each of those means the pipe wall itself is coated, narrowed or invaded. A cable opens a path through the middle, but grease, scale and root fibers stay stuck to the sides and catch the next load of debris.

Jetting removes that coating. The right question is not whether to jet, but whether the pipe is sound enough to jet, which is why we look first.

How It Works

Nozzles, Pressure and Water Flow

A jetter pumps water through a hose to a nozzle with jets pointing backward and sometimes forward. The rear jets pull the hose up the line and blast the pipe wall as it goes. Different nozzles handle different jobs: a general cleaning nozzle for grease, a penetrating nozzle for packed blockages and a rotating root-cutting head for root masses.

Pressure is matched to the pipe. Residential lines take lower pressure than large commercial mains, and a skilled operator adjusts to the pipe material and condition. The loosened debris flushes down to the city main with the water.

Jetting uses nothing but water, so there are no chemicals left in your line.

Cleaning nozzle: grease and sludge.
Penetrating nozzle: packed blockages.
Root-cutting head: root masses at joints.

That fourth row is why a sewer camera inspection comes before jetting on older lines. If the pipe is broken, the fix is repair, not cleaning.

Grease Is a City-Level Concern in Nampa

Nampa's wastewater pretreatment program requires grease interceptors, sized to Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) tables, at food service businesses because fats, oils and grease harden inside sewers and cause blockages and overflows. Homes have no interceptor, so kitchen grease stays in your line until it is removed.

Restaurants and other food businesses on regular jetting schedules avoid far more emergency calls than they pay for in maintenance. Our team for business plumbing sets those schedules. For homes, scraping pans into the trash and running hot water after the disposal does most of the prevention.
