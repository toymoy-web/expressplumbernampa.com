The Honest Answer

When Repair Is Worth It and When It Is Not

We repair when the tank is sound and the failed part is reasonably priced compared with a new unit. A thermocouple, an element, a relief valve or a drain valve all fit that description on a heater with years left in it.

We recommend replacement when the tank shell itself is leaking, because steel that has rusted through cannot be sealed, or when a heater past about ten years needs an expensive part like a gas control valve. In that case a quote on a new heater is free, and you decide with both numbers in front of you.

Repair: thermocouple, flame sensor, elements, thermostats, valves.
Replace: rusted-through tank, or an old tank needing a costly control.
Either way, you see the price before the work.

Hard Water Is Behind Many Nampa Repair Calls

The City of Nampa reports well water hardness ranging from none to about 7 grains per gallon. Homes supplied from the harder wells see scale collect on the bottom of gas tanks and around the lower element in electric tanks.

That scale is why a heater starts popping, why an electric unit keeps burning through lower elements, and why a tank that is only six or seven years old can already be running short on hot water. A yearly flush slows it down.

If you are replacing elements more than once, test the water. A softening system costs less over time than repeated element and anode replacements on a heavily scaled system.

Safety First

What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

  • 1Leaking tank: close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater, then set a gas unit's control to off or switch off an electric unit's breaker.
  • 2Relief valve spraying: do not cap or plug it. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires this valve on every storage water heater because it releases excess temperature or pressure, and blocking it is dangerous. Shut the heater down and call.
  • 3Pilot will not relight: follow the lighting label on the tank once. If it goes out again, stop and let the plumber check the thermocouple or gas valve.
  • 4Gas smell: leave the building, avoid switches and flames, and call Intermountain Gas or 911 from outside.
  • 5Water near electrical: stay out of the area if water reaches outlets or the panel.

Gas vs. Electric

How Gas and Electric Repairs Differ

On a gas water heater, most no-heat calls trace to the ignition side: a pilot that will not stay lit, a thermocouple or flame sensor that no longer reads the flame, or a gas control valve that has failed. Venting and draft also get checked, since a blocked vent can shut some units down for safety.

On an electric heater, the usual suspects are the two heating elements and their thermostats, plus the high-limit switch. A tank with one burnt element still makes some hot water, which is why a common first complaint is hot water that runs out fast rather than none at all.

Both types share the same wear parts outside the burner or elements: the temperature and pressure relief valve, the drain valve and the connections at the top of the tank.

Maintenance

Simple Upkeep That Prevents Repair Calls

Draining a few gallons from the bottom of the tank once a year clears the sediment that Nampa's harder wells leave behind. Lifting the relief valve lever briefly once a year confirms it still opens. Checking the anode rod every few years catches corrosion before it reaches the tank.

Keep the space around a gas heater clear, and never store paint, gasoline or cleaning chemicals near it. For heaters past ten years, compare repair and replacement costs before approving a major part.
