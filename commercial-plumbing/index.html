Local Rules

Nampa's Interceptor Requirements, in Plain Terms

The City of Nampa's wastewater pretreatment program requires grease interceptors for food service businesses and any business where fats, oils or grease can reach the sewer. The city's own list includes restaurants, cafes, fast food outlets, convenience stores, bakeries, bars, schools, churches, hospitals, assisted living facilities, hotels and truck stops.

The same program requires interceptors for sites that can discharge fuel, solvents, oil, sand or grit, such as vehicle wash racks, repair shops, car washes and truck terminals. A hydromechanical grease interceptor needs the Wastewater Superintendent's approval before it goes in, and sizing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) tables for gravity flow rates.

What that means for you: if you are opening, remodeling or changing the use of a space, confirm the interceptor plan early, because it affects floor layout and permit approval. Pumping a full interceptor is a licensed waste hauler's job. The plumber's side is the interceptor installation, the inlet and outlet piping, the baffles and the drain lines that feed it.

Backflow

Backflow Assemblies and Annual Testing

Many commercial buildings have a backflow prevention assembly on the water service, fire line or irrigation connection. Its job is to stop water from the building flowing backward into the public supply, and in Idaho these assemblies are generally expected to be tested every year.

Assemblies fail quietly. Springs weaken, check valves foul with sand or scale, and a unit that was never drained before winter can crack. Our annual assembly testing service covers testing, repair and replacement, and we can explain what your water provider's notice is actually asking for.

Kitchens and Drains

Keeping High-Use Drain Lines Open

Commercial kitchen lines carry far more grease and food waste than a home line, so they clog faster and harder. For a line that keeps closing, scheduled line jetting is usually cheaper over a year than emergency cable calls, because it cleans the full pipe wall instead of punching a hole through the buildup.

Floor drains are the other weak point. A dry floor drain trap lets sewer gas into the space, and a partially blocked one overflows the first time a mop bucket goes in. We clear, camera and repair both, and our drain crews carry the same equipment for commercial calls.

When It Happens

What to Do When a Business Floods or Backs Up

A few minutes of the right steps limits the damage and gets you open sooner.

  • 1Stop the water. Close the fixture's supply valve, or the building main if you cannot isolate the leak. Know where the main is before you need it.
  • 2Stop using the drains. On a backup, close affected restrooms and stop dishwashers and sinks that feed the same line.
  • 3Keep people clear. Block off wet floors and keep staff and customers away from any water near outlets, equipment or panels.
  • 4Do not use chemical openers on a grease line. They rarely clear a commercial clog and they make the line hazardous for the plumber who opens it.
  • 5Record it. Photograph the source and damage and note the time for your insurer, landlord or health inspector.

Water Heaters

Commercial Hot Water That Keeps Up

Restaurants, salons, laundromats and small medical offices use far more hot water than a home, and often in sharp peaks. A residential-size tank that runs cold during the lunch rush is a common and fixable problem.

Commercial water heater sizing looks at peak hourly demand, the temperature each fixture needs and recovery rate. Kitchens that must meet health code dishwashing temperatures may need a booster heater or a dedicated high-temperature supply.

Replacement is permitted work in Nampa, and planning it ahead of a failure avoids a closed kitchen. If your unit is aging, a water heater replacement quote before it fails costs nothing.
