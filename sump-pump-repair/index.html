Sump Pumps

Sump Pump Repair & Installation in Nampa, ID

Sump pump repair in Nampa, Idaho matters more than most people expect for a city with few basements. Many homes here sit over crawlspaces, and in irrigation country the ground under them can get wet every summer. A sump pit with a working pump is what keeps that water out from under the floor.

We repair pumps that have stopped, replace worn-out units, fix discharge lines, and add battery backup so the pump keeps working when the power does not. Emergency calls for a flooding crawlspace or basement are answered 24/7.

Why Here

Irrigation Country Raises the Water Under Your House

For more than a century, farms across the Treasure Valley have been flood-irrigated, and roughly a million acre-feet of water seeps into the ground here each year. Water managers say that irrigation has raised groundwater far above what it would naturally be.

The practical effect shows up in spring. Canal water starts flowing in early April, and the Nampa & Meridian Irrigation District alone delivers to about 69,000 acres. In low-lying homes near Lake Lowell and along canals such as the Ridenbaugh, which runs from Barber Park in Boise to the lake, crawlspace moisture can rise within weeks of that start date.

Canal overflow is a real risk too. In 2025 a Kuna homeowner along Indian Creek reported irrigation water running under his deck and into the storage room beneath the house when the canal opened. A sump pit and pump give that water somewhere to go.

Right Now

What to Do When a Sump Pump Fails

  • 1Check the power first. Make sure the pump is plugged in and its breaker or GFCI outlet has not tripped. Do this only from a dry spot.
  • 2Lift the float gently. If the pump starts when the float rises, the switch is sticking. Free it and watch one full cycle.
  • 3Never stand in water near a pump. Pumps and outlets sit at the lowest point of the house. If water reaches them, stay out and call.
  • 4Move belongings up. Lift stored boxes and anything electrical off the floor of the crawlspace or basement.
  • 5Call before the water spreads. A pump that has failed during a wet stretch will not catch up on its own.
Winter

Frozen Discharge Lines and Power Outages

A sump pump is only as good as the line that carries water away. In Nampa, where the average January low is about 21°F, a discharge line that holds water outside can freeze solid, and the pump then runs against a blocked pipe until it burns out. Sloping the line so it drains, and protecting the exposed outlet, prevents that. Our frozen pipe repair team handles lines that have already frozen.

Power outages are the other weak spot. A battery backup pump takes over when the main pump loses power, and it is worth having if the pit fills quickly in your home.

Discharge should go to the yard or another approved location, well away from the foundation. Routing groundwater into the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed, so the plumber confirms the right place for your property.
