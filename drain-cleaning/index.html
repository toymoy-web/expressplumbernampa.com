That last row matters. Clearing the same line over and over without looking inside it is how a routine cleaning bill turns into a full line replacement. A camera look inside the line shows whether the line is merely dirty or actually damaged.

Tools

Snake, Jet or Camera: Picking the Right Method

A cable machine, often called a snake, drives a spinning cutting head through the pipe. It is the right first tool for most clogs and it opens a line quickly, but it bores a hole through the blockage rather than scrubbing the pipe walls clean.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour grease and scale off the full circumference of the pipe. It suits kitchen lines coated in grease and main lines that keep closing up. It is not the right choice for a fragile, badly cracked pipe, which is one more reason to look with a camera first when the line is old.

Cable machine: fast first response for most single clogs.
Hydro jetting: cleans pipe walls, slows repeat grease clogs.
Camera: shows roots, sags and breaks before anyone guesses.

Nampa Specifics

What Clogs Nampa Drains Most Often

Grease is the most common kitchen culprit here, as it is everywhere. The city takes it seriously enough that Nampa's wastewater pretreatment program requires grease interceptors at restaurants, bakeries, schools and other food businesses, because grease hardens in sewer pipes and causes blockages and overflows. A home kitchen line has no interceptor, so every pan of bacon fat poured down the sink stays in your pipe.

Older homes near downtown Nampa are more likely to have cast iron drains, which roughen inside as they age and catch debris faster than smooth PVC. When those lines keep clogging, the fix is sometimes a section replacement rather than another cleaning, and our sewer repair crews handle that side of the job.

Newer subdivisions have their own pattern. Flushable wipes and paper towels are the usual suspects in lines that are only a few years old, and they tend to snag at the first bend outside the house.

Right Now

What to Do While You Wait for the Plumber

  • 1Stop adding water. Every flush or running tap pushes more water toward the blockage, and on a main line clog it comes up at the lowest drain in the house.
  • 2Skip the chemical drain opener. Caustic products sitting in standing water can burn skin and eyes when the line is opened, and they can damage older pipe.
  • 3Do not plunge a line you already treated. Plunging splashes whatever chemical is in the drain.
  • 4Find the cleanout if you can. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for accessible cleanouts on building drains, and yours is often a capped pipe outside near the foundation or in a crawlspace. Tell the dispatcher where it is so the plumber can go straight to it.
  • 5Check with a neighbor. If the house next door is backing up too, the problem may be in the public sewer main, and the city should be told.

Prevention

Keeping Drains Clear Between Visits

Most drain clogs are built one meal and one shower at a time. Kitchen lines clog from fat, coffee grounds and starchy food. Bathroom lines clog from hair and soap scum, which hard water makes worse because minerals bind soap into a sticky film.

A few habits cut the call rate sharply. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. Put a hair catcher over every shower and tub drain. Run hot water for 15 seconds after using the kitchen sink.

Skip enzyme or chemical drain cleaners as routine maintenance on older pipe. They do little for a real blockage and can be hard on aging cast iron and the gaskets in newer plastic lines.

Wipe grease out of pans before washing.
Hair catchers on every tub and shower.
Hot water flush after kitchen use.
