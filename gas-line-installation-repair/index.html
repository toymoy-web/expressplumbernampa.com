Safety First

What to Do If You Smell Gas

Natural gas has an added odorant that smells like rotten eggs so leaks can be noticed. Treat that smell as an emergency.

  • 1Get everyone out. Leave doors open behind you if it is quick to do so.
  • 2Do not operate switches or devices. No lights, appliances, garage door openers or phones inside the building. A spark can ignite gas.
  • 3Do not try to find the leak. That is the utility's first job.
  • 4Call from a safe distance. Call Intermountain Gas or 911 once you are away from the building.
  • 5Stay out until cleared. Do not go back in until the utility or fire department says it is safe.

Doing It Right

Sizing, Testing and Permits

Gas piping is sized for the total load of every appliance on it and the length of the run, using sizing tables like those in the fuel gas chapter of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). An undersized line starves appliances when several run at once, which shows up as a tankless heater losing heat when the furnace fires. Adding one appliance to an existing system means checking the whole system's capacity, not just the new branch.

We pressure-test every new or repaired gas line before connecting appliances, then leak-check each connection. Flexible corrugated stainless steel tubing (CSST) is common in newer homes, and manufacturers require it to be bonded to the electrical system to reduce lightning damage risk.

Gas piping is licensed, permitted work in Idaho, inspected before it is put into service. Inside Nampa city limits the city issues the permit. Rural properties around Melba, Idaho and other areas without natural gas mains often run on propane, which uses the same careful approach with different regulator and supply details.

Size for total load and run length.
Pressure test before appliances connect.
Bond CSST per the manufacturer.

Appliances and Venting Go Together

A gas line is only half of a safe appliance install. Water heaters and furnaces also need proper venting and combustion air, and a carbon monoxide alarm belongs on every level of a home with gas appliances.

If you are replacing a gas water heater, our heater replacement guide covers the venting side of that job.
