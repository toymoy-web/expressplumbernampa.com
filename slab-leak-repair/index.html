Leaks Under Concrete

Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Nampa, ID

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running under or inside a concrete floor. Many Nampa, Idaho homes sit over crawlspaces, where leaks are easy to see, but slab-on-grade houses, additions and garage slabs hide their pipes in concrete. When one of those lines fails, water can spread under the floor for weeks before anyone notices.

We confirm the leak, pinpoint it through the concrete, and walk you through the three real repair options before anything gets broken.

Sound Familiar?

A Warm Floor, Damp Carpet or Water at the Edge of the Garage Slab

Nobody sees a slab leak directly. They feel a warm patch on the tile, find carpet damp with no spill, hear water running when every tap is off, or notice water at the edge of a garage slab near the foundation.

A warm spot usually means a hot water line is leaking, since that heat travels up through the concrete. A cold, damp area points to the cold line. A water heater sitting on a garage slab adds one more possibility, because a leak at its connections can also track along the slab edge.

The quickest confirmation is the meter test: close every fixture and watch the meter for an hour. Movement with nothing running means water is escaping somewhere, and a slab line is one of the candidates.

Locating

Finding the Leak Without Breaking the Floor

We isolate hot and cold lines separately to learn which one is leaking. Then acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping through the concrete, and thermal imaging traces a hot-water leak by its heat signature on the floor surface.

The goal is a small marked area, not a guess. The general process is the same one described on our hidden leak detection page, with extra steps because concrete muffles sound and spreads heat.

Repair Options

Direct Repair, Reroute or Repipe

Each option has real trade-offs. The right one depends on where the leak is, how old the pipe is and what flooring sits on top.

OptionWhat happensBest whenTrade-off
Direct repairCut the slab at the leak, repair the pipe, patch the concreteOne leak, pipe otherwise sound, accessible flooringFlooring removal and patching at that spot
RerouteAbandon the under-slab line and run new pipe through walls or attic, installed and tested to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC)Pipe is aging or leak is under costly flooringSome wall openings, but no slab cutting
RepipeReplace the home's supply lines entirelyMultiple slab leaks or failing pipe throughoutLargest project, longest-lasting fix

If this is not the first slab leak, rerouting or replacing the supply lines usually beats a second direct repair, because the same pipe tends to fail again nearby.

Right Now

What to Do When You Suspect a Slab Leak

  • 1Close the main if water is visible. Surface water from a slab means a significant leak. Stop the flow first.
  • 2Turn off the water heater if hot water is involved. A warm floor means the heater is refilling constantly and running up energy use.
  • 3Pull back wet rugs and furniture. Let the floor surface breathe and protect belongings.
  • 4Photograph the area and read the meter. Useful for the plumber and for any insurance claim.
  • 5Avoid cutting into the floor yourself. Without locating first, you risk opening the wrong spot.
