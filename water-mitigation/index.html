Water Mitigation in Nampa, ID

Water mitigation is the emergency first stage after water gets into a home: removing standing water, pulling out soaked materials that cannot be saved and setting up drying equipment so the damage stops spreading. For Nampa, Idaho homes, the water damage line at (833) 521-1033 answers 24/7.

Mitigation is about the next few hours and days. It limits how much has to be rebuilt later, and it is the stage insurers expect a homeowner to arrange promptly.

Mitigation vs. Restoration

Two Stages, Two Different Jobs

People use the terms interchangeably, but insurers and restoration crews treat them as separate phases.

The Work

What Happens During Mitigation

Crews start by extracting standing water with pumps and truck-mounted or portable extractors. Wet carpet pad, saturated insulation and drywall that wicked water above the waterline are usually removed, since they rarely dry properly in place.

Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure. Crews take moisture meter readings on walls, subfloors and framing each day, and equipment stays until readings reach normal. Professional crews follow the IICRC S500 standard for water damage work, which sets out how drying is measured and documented.

In crawlspace homes, common around Nampa, mitigation often includes the space under the house: wet insulation removed, standing water pumped out and the crawlspace dried so humidity does not climb into the floors above.

Extract standing water.
Remove materials that will not dry.
Dry and measure daily until normal.
Before the Crew Arrives

What to Do While You Wait

  • 1Stop the source. Close the main shutoff. The pipe itself can be fixed by a plumber, see emergency burst pipe service.
  • 2Avoid electrical hazards. Stay out of standing water near outlets and appliances.
  • 3Document the damage. Photos and video before anything is moved.
  • 4Move valuables up and out. Electronics, documents, photos and rugs first.
  • 5Open windows if weather allows. Fresh air helps in dry weather. In cold weather, keep heat on to aid drying.

Sewer Backups Need Extra Care

Water from a sewer backup is treated as the most contaminated category under the S500 standard. Crews remove porous materials it touched, such as carpet, pad and lower drywall, rather than drying them, then clean and sanitize the surfaces.

Keep children and pets away from the area. If the backup came from the home's own sewer line, it needs fixing before the area is restored, and our crew can repair the damaged sewer line. The same 24/7 water line also covers homes in nearby Middleton and other towns around Nampa.

Insurance and Cost

What Affects the Cost of Mitigation

The affected area, water category and how many days of drying are needed set most of the cost.

Insurers generally expect prompt mitigation after sudden water damage, and delays can affect a claim. Call your insurer early and keep every invoice and moisture log.
