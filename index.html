Local Knowledge

Most Nampa Homes Run on Two Separate Water Systems

Your drinking water comes from the city's own groundwater wells. Your lawn water usually does not. Many Nampa subdivisions have a pressurized irrigation system fed by canals carrying snowmelt from Lucky Peak, Arrowrock and Anderson Ranch reservoirs, delivered through the Nampa & Meridian, Pioneer and Boise-Kuna irrigation districts.

That split changes how a leak gets diagnosed. If your water meter keeps turning with every fixture closed, the leak is on the drinking-water side. If a soggy strip of lawn shows up in May while the meter sits still, suspect the irrigation service line instead.

Ownership matters too. The city maintains the irrigation mains, pump stations and curb stop valve in many subdivisions, but the line from the curb stop to your sprinkler heads is yours. The city will not repair that section, so a leak there is a job for a plumber or irrigation contractor.

The irrigation season typically runs from about mid-April to early October. When it ends, sprinkler lines need to be drained or blown out through your own valve. The city asks residents never to operate its valve, and a line left full is a common source of cracked fittings and backflow problems the following spring.

Before the Plumber Arrives

Emergency Checklist for Nampa Homeowners

  • 1Close the main water shutoff. It is usually where the supply line enters the house, often in the crawlspace, garage or utility room. The city's curb stop by the street is for utility use.
  • 2Shut down the water heater. Turn a gas unit's control to pilot or off, or switch off the breaker for an electric unit, so it does not heat an empty tank.
  • 3Close your own irrigation valve if the leak is outside. Use the personal shutoff on your service line, never the city valve.
  • 4Keep power away from standing water. If water reaches outlets or the electrical panel, stay out of that area and tell the dispatcher.
  • 5Take photos. Capture the source, the damage and any wet ceilings before moving anything, for your insurer or landlord.

Hard Water and Winter Are the Two Local Wildcards

The City of Nampa's water quality report puts hardness anywhere from none to about 7 grains per gallon, depending on which well supplies your area. Homes near the top of that range build scale in tank water heaters and on faucet cartridges, which shortens their service life. A tap test is the cheap first step before deciding on a water softener.

Winter is the other factor. Hose bibs on north walls, supply lines in vented crawlspaces and irrigation lines left full are the first to split when temperatures drop well below freezing. Insulating those three spots each fall prevents most of the frozen-pipe calls we get in January.

Idaho's plumbing code is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, and permitted work in the city is inspected against it.
